# Headnote product principles

## The musician is the author

Every musical idea originates with the musician. Headnote helps capture, understand, reproduce and edit that idea. It does not compose missing parts or invent musical content. Instrument playback renders existing user-created notes; transcription interprets a performance; neither grants permission to generate accompaniment.

## Begin with the smallest useful idea

A voice memo alone can be a project. A text-only notebook entry is also valid. Notes, instrumentation and arrangement emerge when the musician chooses, without mandatory theory or song-structure fields.

Headnote starts with an idea rather than an empty production timeline. Projects can remain simple; multitrack, recording, mixing, effects and MIDI workflows become available as ideas develop into a songwriting workstation.

## Ear first, theory second

Let musicians listen, compare and experiment without needing theoretical terminology. Theory explanations are optional. Users should be able to audition chord types or transpose a note/chord and decide by ear.

## Exploration without automatic composition

Provide tools for user-directed changes: enter a chord, audition it, transpose it, change its quality, inversion or voicing, preview alternatives and compare versions. Apply changes only when the musician explicitly chooses them. Do not decide which chord they should use or generate missing progressions. Descriptors such as tense, open or dreamy are subjective listening aids, not strict theory.

Live computer-keyboard percussion, note input, user-assigned chord/sample triggers and later MIDI devices capture the musician's performance. Recording their timing is allowed user-created input; automatically inventing a rhythm is not.

## Preserve the original source permanently

Original humming, singing, beatboxing and instrument recordings remain immutable source assets. Derived data retains provenance through transcription, edited transcription, instrument interpretation and arrangement. No editing workflow overwrites a source recording.

The musician can listen to the original again, compare it with a transcription, re-transcribe it, reject derived edits and restore earlier versions. Pitch and timing assistance remains controllable and reversible.

## Translate ideas into physically playable music

Help musicians learn and perform what they imagined. Future tools include guitar/bass TAB, piano/keyboard visualisation, staff notation, drum/percussion views, fingering or performance guidance, loops, slowed playback without pitch change and practice mode. Users can record a real performance and mute or replace guide tracks while retaining original sources. This journey from imagined idea to physical performance is a core product goal.

## Make changes to intent visible

Do not silently quantise, tune, harmonise or reinterpret material. Show uncertainty where interpretation is uncertain. Let the musician inspect and correct it; explanations must help them decide rather than imply the software knows their intent.

## One musical idea, several representations

Canonical musical information is independent of piano roll, TAB and sheet music. Instrument-specific views derive from it and communicate constraints or ambiguity. An edit in a representation updates underlying information through explicit application operations, not a separate competing musical model.

## No default instrument worldview

Guitar is one supported instrument among many. Navigation, project structure and musical data must also serve voice, bass, piano, synth, strings, rhythm and other instruments.

## Keep capture and retrieval approachable

Titles, free-form notes, user-defined tags and search support musicians who do not know notation or theory. Use clear language, practical keyboard navigation and honest feedback about saving and errors.

## Grow through tested increments

Build only the requested feature. Keep each stage useful, test its behavior and avoid dependencies or abstractions justified only by speculative future needs. Preserve clear architectural boundaries without building every future subsystem in advance.
